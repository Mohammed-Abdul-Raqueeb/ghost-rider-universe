    Publication history

    COMIC BOOK
    HISTORY

    Ten volumes of the main title, a dozen spin-offs and a publishing life that mirrors every fashion in American comics from the 1970s horror boom to the 2020s event machine.

    THE 1970s

    Horror comes to Marvel

    The 1971 revision of the Comics Code re-admitted vampires, ghouls and werewolves, and Marvel built a horror line overnight. Ghost Rider debuted in Marvel Spotlight #5, moved to his own book in 1973 and stayed there for a decade — through the Champions (1975–78), team-ups with Spider-Man, Daredevil and the Thing, and a steady drift from monster-of-the-month stories toward the character study that ended the run.

    J.M. DeMatteis's closing issues (#77–81, 1983) named and expelled Zarathos and gave Johnny Blaze a rare thing in superhero comics: a real ending.

    THE 1990s

    Ketch, Midnight Sons and the boom

    The 1990 relaunch by Mackie and Saltares hit at the peak of the speculator market. Ghost Rider vol. 3 was a top-selling book; it spawned Ghost Rider/Blaze: Spirits of Vengeance (1992–94), Blaze (1994–95), Ghost Rider 2099 (1994–96), Midnight Sons Unlimited, and the whole Midnight Sons imprint of supernatural titles. The 1992 Rise of the Midnight Sons crossover and 1993's Siege of Darkness were line-wide events.

    By the late 1990s the market had collapsed; the series ended in 1998 with its final issue unpublished for nearly a decade.

    THE 2000s

    Marvel Knights and the Ennis reset

    A six-issue Marvel Knights series in 2001 (Devin Grayson, Trent Kaniuga) and Garth Ennis's painted Road to Damnation (2005) and Trail of Tears (2007) with Clayton Crain restored the character's horror credentials just as the first film arrived. Daniel Way's 2006 ongoing and Jason Aaron's run from #20 (2008) rebuilt the mythology — Zadkiel, Heaven's design, an army of Riders — and remain the most acclaimed modern stretch.

    THE 2010s

    New hosts

    Marvel tried a new host twice in three years: Alejandra Jones (2011) and Robbie Reyes (2014). All-New Ghost Rider ran twelve issues; Robbie returned for a 2016–17 series, then joined Jason Aaron's Avengers in 2018. Cosmic Ghost Rider (2017) became an unexpected hit, headlining three miniseries. Damnation (2018) put Johnny Blaze on the throne of Hell, and the 2019–20 series dealt with the consequences.

    THE 2020s

    Percy, Pirzada and the Violent Era

    Benjamin Percy's Ghost Rider (2022–24) was the longest Blaze-led run since the 1970s, extended by the Weapons of Vengeance crossover with Wolverine, Final Vengeance (2024) and Vengeance Forever. Sabir Pirzada's Spirits of Vengeance (2024–25) and Spirits of Violence (2025–26) gathered every Rider into one story. Marvel's collected-edition programme — Omnibuses, Epic Collections and a Percy Omnibus scheduled for 2026 — has meanwhile put the whole history back in print.
